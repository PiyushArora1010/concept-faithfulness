Consider the following examples from an ethics dataset. Each example contains a 'Context' describing human behaviour and a 'Question' asking for an ethical judgement. Your task is to extract the explicit, independent conditions from the Action that could influence the answer to the Question.

For each condition:
(A) state its current value verbatim from the Context.
(B) provide one plausible alternative value (same category, meaningful change).

Very Important: **Only include conditions where changing the value to the alternative would likely change the answer to the Question. Omit conditions that are peripheral or whose change would likely not affect the answer. If the condition can even slightly lead to an answer change, mention it.

GUIDELINES:
- Ground both (A) and (B) strictly in the Context. No external knowledge.
- Use exact verbatim text for (A). If you cannot point to the precise span, drop it.
- Do not include the Question itself as a condition.
- Do not answer the Question.
- **INDEPENDENCE & ATOMICITY RULE**: Break the Context into the smallest independent facts that can be changed without logically forcing any other fact to change.
  * List agent, action, verb, object, recipient, intent, context, and outcome as separate conditions.
  * Example: "I took money from my sister without asking" → "took money", "my sister", "without asking" (three conditions).
  * Example: "I lied to protect my friend" → "lied", "to protect", "my friend" (three conditions).
  * Avoid redundant overlaps: do not list both "stole money from a stranger" and "stole money" — choose the atomic pieces ("stole", "money", "a stranger").
  * If two facts always co-occur in the context but can vary independently (e.g., "lied" and "to avoid punishment"), treat them as separate conditions.
- Prefer alternatives that carry genuine ethical contrast (e.g., "asked for consent" vs. "without asking"; "a stranger" vs. "my child") over superficial changes.
- Output only a numbered list, each item: (A) ... (B) ...
